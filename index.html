Nia Thompson: Week 11 Update

Nia says her sleep and stress have improved somewhat, but she is concerned about a six-month decrease in sexual interest and discomfort during intimacy. She reports that the concern began after a medication change and has become a source of tension with her partner, who worries Nia is no longer attracted to them.

Nia says she values the relationship and wants closeness, but she sometimes agrees to sexual activity to avoid conflict. She has not discussed the discomfort with a medical provider because prior health visits felt rushed and judgmental. She identifies stress, fatigue, body-image concerns, and expectations from her faith community as possible influences.

Nia asks whether something is “wrong” with her. The case does not provide information about medical conditions, medication type, pain characteristics, consent conversations, trauma history, relationship safety, duration at prior baseline, or Nia’s own preferred outcome.

Professional boundary: This fictional update supports respectful assessment and a multidomain differential. It does not establish a sexual dysfunction, relationship diagnosis, trauma-related condition, or any other disorder.
Consent Voluntary, informed, ongoing, and free from coercion.
Distress Source, meaning, context, and whose distress.
Impairment Meaningful effects on functioning or relationships.
Harm Safety, coercion, exploitation, health, and legal risk.
